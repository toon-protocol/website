A connector is ordinarily a clearnet host. It can also be a hidden service: a node reachable only inside the Anyone Protocol overlay, at a .anyone address that public DNS cannot resolve and no certificate authority can certify. Every spaceturtle node is one, and so is the anytoon credentials issuer.

Paying such a node needs one extra thing: a running anon daemon, and its SOCKS5h port.

  you ──► anon (SOCKS5h) ──► the overlay ──► connector.anyone ──► app
              ▲
              └── chain RPC goes here too, not around it

Nothing else changes. Sealing, pricing, vouchers, channels and refusals are identical; the packet does not know what carried it.

From the CLI

Nothing, if you let it: point toon at a .anyone address and it starts a daemon for you.

export TOON_CONNECTOR=http://<address>.anyone
npx toon describe
stderr
toon: http://<address>.anyone is a hidden service; starting a local anon daemon
toon: downloading anon-live-linux-amd64.zip (v0.4.10.2)…
toon: verified; extracting
toon: starting anon v0.4.10.2 on 127.0.0.1:41337 (…/anon)
toon: anon is listening; building circuits on demand
toon: proxying through socks5h://127.0.0.1:41337

Every line of that goes to stderr, never stdout, so --json output stays exactly one parseable document. Already running your own daemon? Name it, and nothing is downloaded and no process is spawned:

npx toon send --socks socks5h://127.0.0.1:9050 --body 'hello'
export TOON_SOCKS=socks5h://127.0.0.1:9050     # same thing, for every command

A clearnet connector starts no daemon and downloads nothing.

The managed daemon

An operator is entitled to know what would run on their machine.

Releasev0.4.10.2, pinned, on the stable live channel.
Platformslinux-x64, linux-arm64, darwin-x64, darwin-arm64, win32-x64.
Verificationsha256 against a checksum recorded in the client's repository, checked before the binary is ever executed. A platform with no pinned checksum is refused, not downloaded and trusted.
Cache$XDG_CACHE_HOME/toon/anon/<version>/, falling back to ~/.toon/anon/<version>/. Keyed by version, so a new pin never silently reuses an old binary.

What a hidden service does and does not buy

A hidden-service endpoint hides where the node is reachable, and nothing else. The chain still sees the channel's deposit and settlement, and the voucher still names the paying key. A node at a .anyone address needs no domain, no public IP and no open port, which is why spaceturtle can run one from a laptop behind a home router.

Running one

On the node's side, the connector publishes its .anyone address as its endpoint and reaches onion peers through its own socks_proxy. The connector repository's onion endpoint bring-up is the operator runbook, and spaceturtle does all of it for you.
